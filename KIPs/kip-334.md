## Abstract

When a proposer fails or messages are delayed and consensus does not complete, Kaia BFT starts a new round at the same block height. In the existing protocol, a validator that observes a quorum of votes for a block considers the block prepared and sets a local lock on it, which keeps it from voting for a conflicting block in later rounds. However, the round change that Kaia inherited from IBFT does not convey the lock or the votes behind it to the next proposer, so the proposer may propose a conflicting block and the height can stall.

This KIP defines a prepared certificate, a block with the signed `PREPARE` or `COMMIT` votes proving that it was prepared. A `ROUND CHANGE` carries a signed claim on its sender's certificate together with the certificate itself. The proposer of the new round re-proposes the block prepared in the highest round with its certificate, and the other validators accept the proposal regardless of their own locks once they verify that certificate, which preserves any block that could have been decided.

## Motivation

While preparing the transition to permissionless validator participation, Kaia re-examined the round-change procedure of its BFT consensus against the Istanbul-family protocols established since, and found that a lock in Kaia BFT is kept only locally. The issue is not new to the permissionless transition, but the transition is the occasion to address it with an explicit protocol rule.

A locked validator rejects any proposal at the same height that differs from its locked block. Honest validators can therefore end up locked on different blocks, and a single Byzantine validator can then stall the height. Consider four validators `A`, `B`, `C`, and `D` with a quorum of three, where `D` is Byzantine.

1. In round 0, all four vote for block `X`, but because of network delays only `A` observes the quorum and locks on `X`.
2. In round 1, an unlocked proposer proposes block `Y`. `A` rejects it, and `B`, `C`, and `D` vote for it. Only `C` observes the quorum and locks on `Y`.
3. From then on, `A` rejects every proposal other than `X`, and `C` every proposal other than `Y`. If `D` stops voting, no proposal gathers three votes, and the height is never decided.

[PBFT](#references) solves this problem in its view change, and [IBFT 2.0 and QBFT](#references) bring it to the Istanbul family as the round change: a prepared block travels with its proof, and the next proposer re-proposes the block prepared in the highest round. Rather than adopting any of these protocols as a whole, this KIP introduces their common recovery principle into Kaia BFT. In the example above, every round-change quorum then includes the claim of `A` or `C`, so an honest proposer re-proposes the claimed block with its certificate. Both locked validators accept it, and `A`, `B`, and `C` form a quorum.

## Specification

The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT", "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT", "RECOMMENDED", "MAY", and "OPTIONAL" in this document are to be interpreted as described in RFC 2119.

### Terminology

| Term | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Height** (`Sequence`) | The number of the block being decided. |
| **Round** | An attempt to decide a block at a height. Each height starts at round 0, and a failed round is followed by a later round at the same height. |
| **View** | A pair of a round and a height. |
| **Digest** | The hash of a proposed block. It excludes the round recorded in the header and the committed seals, so the same block has the same digest in every round. It includes the proposer's seal, so a re-proposed block keeps the seal, and the author, of the proposer that first proposed it. |
| **Committee** | The validators eligible to vote at a height. From the Permissionless fork, the committee is the same in every round of a height. `n` denotes its size. |
| **Proposer** | The committee member designated to propose a block in a view. |
| **Quorum** | `⌈2n/3⌉` votes from distinct committee members, or `n` if `n < 4`. |
| **f** | The number of Byzantine committee members tolerated, `⌈n/3⌉ − 1`. |
| **Prepared** | A block is prepared in a round when a validator observes `PREPARE` or `COMMIT` votes for it from a quorum in that round. |
| **Lock** | The prepared block a validator keeps for the current height, together with the round in which it was prepared. It persists across rounds until it is replaced or released as described in [Entering the prepared state](#entering-the-prepared-state). |
| **Committed seal** | A validator's signature, carried in its `COMMIT`, attesting that it committed a block. A quorum of committed seals is recorded in the header of the decided block. |

### Message formats

Every consensus message is sent in a `Message` envelope. The envelope's `Code` identifies the message type, and its `Msg` carries the payload of that type.

This KIP adds two proofs to these messages. A `ROUND CHANGE` proves which block its sender has prepared, and a `PRE-PREPARE` above round 0 proves that its proposal respects the blocks prepared in earlier rounds.

| Code | Message | Payload in `Msg` | Proof carried |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | `PRE-PREPARE` | `Preprepare` | justification: `RoundChangeCertificate` and `PreparedMessages`, in `Msg` |
| 1 | `PREPARE` | `Prepare` | — |
| 2 | `COMMIT` | `Commit` | — |
| 3 | `ROUND CHANGE` | `RoundChange` | prepared claim in `Msg`, and the prepared certificate it summarizes as `Evidence` |

A prepared certificate is a block with the `PREPARE` or `COMMIT` envelopes proving that the block was prepared in a given round. A `ROUND CHANGE` sender signs only a compact claim on its certificate and attaches the certificate itself as the unsigned `Evidence` of the envelope.

A round-change certificate (RCC) is a set of signed `ROUND CHANGE` envelopes from at least a quorum, stripped of their `Evidence`, for the round of a `PRE-PREPARE`. Together with `PreparedMessages`, the votes behind the claim that the proposal re-proposes, it forms the justification of the `PRE-PREPARE`.

The following conventions apply to all formats in this section.

- All messages are RLP-encoded.
- An optional field is omitted from the encoding when it has no value.
- Following the existing Istanbul encoding, `View` is encoded as `[Round, Sequence]`, with the round first.

#### Message envelope

```text
Message {
    PrevHash    // parent block hash of the height being decided
    Code        // message type
    Msg         // RLP-encoded payload
    Address     // sender
    Signature   // signature of the sender
    Evidence    // optional, ROUND CHANGE only
}
```

`Signature` is the sender's ECDSA signature over `Keccak256(RLP([PrevHash, Code, Msg, Address]))`. `Evidence` is not part of the signed preimage, so removing `Evidence` from a signed envelope leaves the signature valid. Instead, `Evidence` MUST match the `EvidenceHash` inside the signed `Msg`, so a relay cannot replace the attached evidence with a different value.

The following rules apply to `Evidence`.

- `Evidence` MAY be present only in a `ROUND CHANGE` message. A message of any other code that carries `Evidence` is invalid.
- When there is no `Evidence`, the field MUST be omitted. A message that explicitly encodes a zero-length `Evidence` is invalid. Allowing it would give the same message two valid encodings, which could be used to bypass gossip deduplication.

#### PREPARE and COMMIT messages

`PREPARE` and `COMMIT` are the votes of a round. They are also the elements of a prepared certificate and of `PreparedMessages`.

```text
Prepare {
    View
    Digest
}

Commit {
    View
    Digest
    CommittedSeal   // signature of the sender over Digest || 0x02 || byte(View.Round)
}
```

The envelope `Signature` and `CommittedSeal` are 65-byte secp256k1 ECDSA signatures encoded as `R (32 bytes) || S (32 bytes) || V (1 byte)`, where `V` is either `0` or `1`.

`CommittedSeal` is part of `Msg` and is therefore also authenticated by the envelope signature. It is copied as is into the header of the committed block. The header records the decided round in the last byte of the vanity area of `Extra`, which the block hash excludes, so the committed seal binds the round instead.

- `CommittedSeal` is a signature over `Keccak256(Digest || 0x02 || byte(View.Round))`, where `0x02` is the message code of `COMMIT` and `byte(View.Round)` is the low 8 bits of the round.
- On receiving a `COMMIT`, a validator recovers the signer of `CommittedSeal` over this preimage and rejects the message if the signer differs from the sender.
- When verifying a block header, a node recovers the signer of each committed seal with the header's round byte in the preimage. If the round byte differs from the round in which the seals were made, the quorum check on the committed seals fails and the block is invalid.
- A validator that receives a `PRE-PREPARE` for an already decided height MAY respond with a `COMMIT` when the block is in its local chain and the sender is the proposer of that view, but only if the round of the `PRE-PREPARE` equals the round recorded in the stored header. Otherwise it would produce a committed seal for a round in which it never committed.

#### Prepared certificate

```text
PreparedCertificate {
    View        // [Round, Sequence] in which Proposal was prepared
    Proposal    // prepared block
    Messages    // signed PREPARE or COMMIT envelopes from at least a quorum
}
```

A prepared certificate proves that `Proposal` reached the prepared state in `View`. A certificate is not a standalone consensus message; it is carried as `RLP(PreparedCertificate)` in the `Evidence` of a `ROUND CHANGE`. A valid prepared certificate satisfies all of the following conditions.

- The block number of `Proposal` equals `View.Sequence`, and the header of `Proposal` carries no committed seals.
- The body of `Proposal` passes the block body checks that precede execution: it matches the transactions root of the header, and its size, transaction gas prices, and blob sidecars satisfy the block rules. The votes bind only the digest, which does not cover every part of the body.
- Each element of `Messages` is a `PREPARE` or `COMMIT` envelope without `Evidence`. The `View` of each payload equals the `View` of the certificate, and its `Digest` equals the hash of `Proposal`. The `PrevHash` of each envelope equals the parent hash of `Proposal`.
- The signature of each envelope is a signature of its `Address`. For a `COMMIT`, `CommittedSeal` is also a signature of its `Address`.
- Every sender is a committee member and appears at most once, with either its `PREPARE` or its `COMMIT`. The number of `Messages` does not exceed the committee size, and the number of senders is at least the quorum.

#### ROUND CHANGE message

```text
RoundChange {
    View
    Prepared {          // optional
        Round           // View.Round of the certificate
        Digest          // hash of the certificate's Proposal
        EvidenceHash    // Keccak256(Evidence)
    }
}
```

`View` is the round and sequence the sender is moving to. `Prepared` is a claim summarizing the prepared certificate the sender holds for the same sequence, and is omitted when the sender holds none. Its `EvidenceHash` binds the signed claim to the unsigned certificate.

A valid `ROUND CHANGE` message satisfies all of the following conditions.

- `Prepared` and `Evidence` are either both present or both absent. When `Prepared` is present, `Keccak256(Evidence)` equals `Prepared.EvidenceHash`.
- `Prepared.Round` is less than `View.Round`, and `Prepared.Digest` is not the zero hash.
- The certificate decoded from `Evidence` is a valid prepared certificate. Its `View` is `[Prepared.Round, View.Sequence]`, and the hash of its `Proposal` equals `Prepared.Digest`.

#### PRE-PREPARE message

```text
Preprepare {
    View
    Proposal
    RoundChangeCertificate   // optional, signed ROUND CHANGE envelopes
    PreparedMessages         // optional, signed PREPARE or COMMIT envelopes
}
```

The header of `Proposal` carries no committed seals. A round-0 `PRE-PREPARE` MUST carry neither `RoundChangeCertificate` nor `PreparedMessages`. A `PRE-PREPARE` for a round greater than 0 MUST carry `RoundChangeCertificate`, and justifies its `Proposal` with these two fields, which together are called the justification of the `PRE-PREPARE`.

`RoundChangeCertificate` is a list of at least a quorum of signed `ROUND CHANGE` envelopes for `View`. `Evidence` MUST be removed from each envelope, which leaves its signature valid. Every sender is a committee member and appears at most once, and the `PrevHash` of each envelope equals the parent hash of the current height. For each claim, `Prepared.Round` is less than `View.Round` and `Prepared.Digest` is not the zero hash, as in [ROUND CHANGE message](#round-change-message).

`PreparedMessages` is the list of votes proving the prepared claim in `RoundChangeCertificate` that `Proposal` re-proposes, and the proposer omits it when `Proposal` re-proposes no claim. In case 2 of [PRE-PREPARE processing](#pre-prepare-processing), a receiver reconstructs a prepared certificate from `[Round of that claim, View.Sequence]`, `Proposal`, and `PreparedMessages`, and verifies it; in case 3, it ignores `PreparedMessages`. How a proposer selects a claim and how a receiver verifies the justification are defined in [Proposal selection](#proposal-selection) and [PRE-PREPARE processing](#pre-prepare-processing).

### Consensus flow

Within a round, a validator is in one of the following states. The states and their transitions are those of the existing Istanbul BFT consensus; the last column lists what this KIP adds or changes.

| State | The validator | Leaves when | Added by this KIP |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `AcceptRequest` | Waits for the `PRE-PREPARE` of the current round. The proposer builds and sends one. | It accepts a valid `PRE-PREPARE` and sends a `PREPARE` → `Preprepared` | Above round 0, verifies the justification first. A `PRE-PREPARE` with an invalid justification moves it to round change at once. |
| `Preprepared` | Has accepted a proposal and collects `PREPARE` and `COMMIT` votes for it. | Votes from a quorum arrive, and it sends a `COMMIT` → `Prepared` | A locked validator also waits for a quorum in this round; a lock from an earlier round does not substitute for it. |
| `Prepared` | Is locked on the proposal and collects `COMMIT` votes. | `COMMIT` from a quorum arrives → `Committed` | Stores the prepared certificate together with the lock. |
| `Committed` | Executes the block and inserts it into its chain. | The block of the current height is inserted, here or in any other state → `AcceptRequest` of the next height | — |
| Round change | Has sent a `ROUND CHANGE` in any state when the round timer expires or it rejects a `PRE-PREPARE`, and waits for a quorum of them. | A quorum for one round arrives → `AcceptRequest` of that round. If the timer expires again, it sends a `ROUND CHANGE` for the round after. | Carries its prepared certificate in `ROUND CHANGE`, and keeps the quorum as the round-change certificate. |

The following subsections follow the life of a prepared certificate: a validator creates one when it enters the prepared state, carries it in a `ROUND CHANGE`, the proposer of the next round selects one from the round-change certificate, and the other validators verify that selection when they process the `PRE-PREPARE`.

#### Entering the prepared state

When a validator accepts the `PRE-PREPARE` of a proposal in its current view, it collects `PREPARE` and `COMMIT` messages for the same view and the hash of that proposal. A `PREPARE` and a `COMMIT` from the same sender count as one. When the votes reach a quorum, the validator:

1. considers the proposal prepared and locks on it in the current round;
2. stores the proposal and the votes it has received for it as its prepared certificate for the current view; and
3. sends a `COMMIT` if it has not already done so.

A locked validator that receives the `PRE-PREPARE` of its locked block in a later round sends a `PREPARE` and waits for a quorum in that round, as it would for any other proposal. This follows [PBFT](#references), in which replicas run the prepare phase again for every value re-proposed in a new view, and [QBFT](#references). Hence, whenever an honest validator sends a `COMMIT` in a round, it holds a prepared certificate for that round. The only exception is a `COMMIT` for an already decided block, described in [PREPARE and COMMIT messages](#prepare-and-commit-messages).

The lock and the prepared certificate change only in the following cases.

- When the validator observes a quorum in a later round, the lock and certificate of that round replace the earlier ones. Typically the later round re-proposes the locked block, and only the round of the lock advances.
- When a certificate verified in [PRE-PREPARE processing](#pre-prepare-processing) has a higher round than the one it holds, the validator adopts that certificate.
- When the validator executes its locked block, either on importing it after a commit or in case 3 of [PRE-PREPARE processing](#pre-prepare-processing), and the execution fails, it records the block as invalid and releases both the lock and the certificate. Claims on that block are void for it from then on.
- When the validator moves to a new height, it releases both the lock and the certificate.

#### Round change

When its round timer expires or it rejects a `PRE-PREPARE`, a validator moves to the next round of the same height and sends a `ROUND CHANGE` for that round. If it holds a prepared certificate, the `ROUND CHANGE` claims it and carries it as `Evidence`.

A validator accepts a `ROUND CHANGE` that is valid as defined in [ROUND CHANGE message](#round-change-message) and comes from a committee member. It keeps one `ROUND CHANGE` per sender for each round, replacing it only with one that has a strictly higher claim.

When a validator has `ROUND CHANGE` messages for the same round from a quorum of committee members, it starts that round and keeps those messages as the round-change certificate of that round. As before, `ROUND CHANGE` messages for a higher round from `f + 1` committee members make a validator that is waiting for a round change move to that round and send its own `ROUND CHANGE`.

#### Proposal selection

A claim is void for a validator when it claims a block that the validator has executed and found invalid. The proposer of a round greater than 0 re-proposes the prepared block with the highest claim in the round-change certificate that started the round, as selected by `SelectCertificate`.

```text
function SelectCertificate(sequence, round, RCC):
    claims ← prepared claims in RCC, except void claims
    if own lock is set and its round is higher than that of every claim in claims:
        replace own ROUND CHANGE in RCC, or add one if RCC has none, with a newly signed one that claims own prepared certificate
        return own prepared certificate
    if claims is not empty:
        return prepared certificate received with a claim of the highest round in claims
    return none
```

If no certificate is selected, the proposer proposes a new block. It broadcasts the `PRE-PREPARE` with its justification, and MUST NOT send a `PRE-PREPARE` for the round with any other proposal.

#### PRE-PREPARE processing

A validator processes a `PRE-PREPARE` from the proposer of its current view under the existing rules. In addition, the `PRE-PREPARE` MUST be valid as defined in [PRE-PREPARE message](#pre-prepare-message), and for a round greater than 0 the validator accepts its proposal only in the cases below.

The cases depend on the claims in the round-change certificate. Void claims are ignored, but their senders still count toward the quorum.

1. No claim remains. A block decided in an earlier round would leave a claim in every round-change certificate, so no block can have been decided, and the proposal may be any block. `PreparedMessages` MUST be empty.
2. The proposal is a block claimed at the highest remaining round. The prepared certificate reconstructed from that round, the sequence, the proposal, and `PreparedMessages` MUST be valid. The justification then proves this certificate.
3. The proposal is any other block. Either the proposer treated the highest claim as void, which the validator may not know, or the highest claim is not backed by a valid prepared certificate. The validator decides as follows.

    ```text
    if own lock is not set:
        accept                          // no block of its own to protect
    else:
        execute the locked block, unless it has already been executed at this height
        if the execution fails:         // the block is now known invalid and the lock is released
            check the justification again, with claims on that block now void, and decide again from case 1
        else:
            reject
    ```

If the `PRE-PREPARE` is invalid or its proposal is not accepted in any of these cases, the validator rejects it and sends a `ROUND CHANGE` for the next round. Otherwise the validator verifies the proposal as a block, as before, and accepts the `PRE-PREPARE` regardless of its own lock. If the justification proves a certificate with a higher round than the one the validator holds, the validator adopts that certificate and its block as its lock; otherwise it keeps its lock until a quorum in this round replaces it. The validator then sends a `PREPARE` and proceeds as in [Entering the prepared state](#entering-the-prepared-state).

### Activation

This KIP is activated by the Permissionless hard fork. Its rules apply from the Permissionless fork block onward, and the existing rules apply before it.

## Rationale

The recovery mechanism of this KIP is the view change of [PBFT](#references), in the round-change form of [QBFT](#references). The first two subsections explain rules that this KIP takes from QBFT and that differ from the existing Kaia BFT; the remaining ones explain where this KIP differs from QBFT to fit Kaia.

### Signed claim and unsigned certificate

As in [QBFT](#references), a `ROUND CHANGE` signs only a claim on its prepared certificate and carries the certificate outside the signature, so that a `PRE-PREPARE` can embed the same signed `ROUND CHANGE` without the certificate and carry the selected block and its votes only once. The justification of a `PRE-PREPARE`, in contrast, is placed inside `Msg`, because a `PRE-PREPARE` is never embedded in another message. Unlike QBFT, the signed claim includes `EvidenceHash`, which binds the attached certificate to the claim, so a relay cannot attach a different certificate to the same claim.

### Accepting a justified proposal regardless of the lock

A locked validator accepts a justified `PRE-PREPARE` even if its proposal differs from its locked block, including a block claimed at a round lower than its own lock. This follows QBFT, and matches PBFT, which has no lock and in which a backup accepts a new view by checking its view-change messages alone. By the quorum-intersection argument of the PBFT view change, if a block has been decided, every round-change certificate contains a claim on it from an honest validator, and every valid prepared certificate of a higher round is also for that block. In case 2 of [PRE-PREPARE processing](#pre-prepare-processing), the validator verifies the prepared certificate of the highest claim, so a proposal accepted in that case keeps a decided block without a comparison with the local lock. Such a comparison would only stall the height when validators locked at a round higher than every claim in the certificate refuse the proposal.

The highest signed claim in a round-change certificate is not necessarily backed by a valid prepared certificate, because the embedded `ROUND CHANGE` messages carry no `Evidence`. A Byzantine proposer can sign a claim at a higher round without a certificate and include it in its own round-change certificate. Such a proposer cannot satisfy case 2 for that claim, and any other proposal falls into case 3, where the locks of honest validators keep a decided block, as explained in [Void claims on invalid blocks](#void-claims-on-invalid-blocks).

### COMMIT votes in a certificate

QBFT forms a prepared certificate from `PREPARE` messages only. Kaia enters the prepared state on a quorum of `PREPARE` and `COMMIT` messages combined, so a certificate contains the votes that formed that quorum, of either kind.

### Void claims on invalid blocks

In Kaia, a block is executed only after it is committed, when it is inserted into the chain, so even a committed block can fail execution. When it does, the validator records the block as invalid and releases its lock on it, and treats any claim on it as void. Otherwise the highest-prepared-round rule would re-propose the block in every later round, and the height would never be decided. Since validators find a block invalid at different times, a validator without a lock accepts a proposal below a claim, and a locked validator accepts it only after its own locked block fails execution. This keeps a decided block: at least a quorum less `f` honest validators lock it and never find it invalid, so the remaining validators are fewer than a quorum and cannot form one for another block.

### Round-bound committed seal

The safety arguments of PBFT and QBFT assume that a committed block had a `COMMIT` quorum in a single view or round. Without the round in the committed seal, seals for the same block from different rounds could be combined into a valid header, although no single round reached a `COMMIT` quorum.

## Backwards Compatibility

This KIP is a hard fork and is not backward compatible. All nodes MUST upgrade to a client implementing this KIP before the Permissionless fork block, not only validators: the round-bound committed seal changes how every node verifies a block header, so a node running an older client rejects blocks from the fork block onward. Validators additionally exchange the consensus messages defined in this KIP. Blocks before the fork block are processed under the existing rules.

## Security Considerations

### Cost of verifying `Evidence`

`Evidence` is block-sized and expensive to verify. Implementations SHOULD check `EvidenceHash` and the sender before decoding `Evidence`, cache verified certificates, and bound the number of future rounds retained. A `ROUND CHANGE` for a round beyond this bound is discarded, so it counts neither toward starting that round nor toward the `f + 1` messages that make a waiting validator catch up. The bound is implementation-specific and does not affect safety. Since the round timer doubles with each round, honest validators do not fall that many rounds apart in practice.

### Executing the locked block

A Byzantine proposer can repeatedly propose a block below a claim to make locked validators execute their locked blocks. A validator executes each locked block at most once per height, so the cost is bounded by the number of distinct blocks it locks at the height. A failure unrelated to the block itself, such as unavailable parent state, gives no result, and the block is executed again on the next occasion.

### Round byte in the committed seal

The committed seal includes only the low 8 bits of the round, so seals from rounds `r` and `r + 256` share a preimage. A height does not reach 256 rounds in practice, nor even round 34. A round with a Byzantine proposer can end as soon as honest validators reject its `PRE-PREPARE`. In a round with an honest proposer, however, honest validators move on before the round timer expires only if they cannot accept the valid proposal, for example because their state is temporarily unavailable. The round timer doubles with each round and reaches 2<sup>33</sup> seconds, over 270 years, at round 33. The proposer of each round follows an order of the committee shuffled with the mix hash of the parent block, so long runs of consecutive rounds with Byzantine proposers are unlikely. Barring local failures, reaching round 34 would require a round with an honest proposer at round `33 − k` or later to time out, where `k` is the longest such run, which takes more than 2<sup>33 − k</sup> seconds, or over three months even for `k = 10`.

### Proposals below a claim

In case 3 of [PRE-PREPARE processing](#pre-prepare-processing), a validator without a lock accepts a proposal below the highest claim without verifying the claim, so a Byzantine proposer can make unlocked validators vote for an arbitrary block. Safety does not depend on them, as explained in [Void claims on invalid blocks](#void-claims-on-invalid-blocks); at worst the round fails, as with any faulty proposer.

### Message size

A `ROUND CHANGE` with `Evidence` carries a whole block and up to `n` votes. A `PRE-PREPARE` above round 0 carries a block, up to `n` `ROUND CHANGE` envelopes, and up to `n` votes. Both must fit in a single consensus message of the P2P layer, which is limited to 12 MiB. The block size limit of 10 MiB, in force since the Osaka hard fork, leaves the remaining 2 MiB for the envelopes, each of which is a few hundred bytes.

### Committed seals in a proposal

The block hash excludes committed seals, so copies of a proposal that differ only in committed seals share a digest and pass the same vote checks. Rejecting proposals and certificates that carry committed seals prevents such padded copies from being retained, re-proposed, or relayed.
